Marches

Raise troops and march them across a land of provinces.

Five realms share a land of plains, forests, hills and mountains, cut by a river that troops cross only at its bridges. Each realm taxes its provinces, raises troops and sends them to settle, take and defend land. Troops walk on the same daily clock as everything else: allies share a province, enemies fight where they meet. You rule one realm; the Lilienstern engine runs the others, in your browser.

How to play

  1. Click a province to see who holds it, its terrain, castles and wealth, the troops in it, and the decisions you can take there: settle empty land next to yours, govern, fortify, raise troops (in a province you hold), or make war and peace with the realm that holds it.
  2. Click one of your troops (on the map, or in a province's card), then click where it should go. It takes the quickest way: hills, forests and mountains are slow, horses and tanks cannot climb everywhere, and the river can only be crossed at bridges. You can give orders as often as you like; decisions are limited to a few a day.
  3. Troops that meet enemies stop and fight, every day, until one side is gone: each troop hits the enemy it is best against. Defenders are helped by hills, forests, castles and their own land.
  4. A troop standing in a province with no enemy troops occupies it: day by day its realm's hold grows and the old holder's fades, and the province changes hands. Heavy troops take places fastest; castles slow them down.
  5. Every province pays funds and gives recruits each day, as far as its people are content; the Treasury shows where every coin came from and went. Troops cost funds every day, paid first thing in the morning. There is no limit to an army but its cost: raise more than your land can feed and the treasury empties, unpaid troops lose health and plunder your own provinces, taxes fall, and angry provinces revolt.
  6. The people (the Happiness view): battles, troops quartered on them, levies and war taxes make them unhappy, and so does a very big realm; left alone they calm down. Feed the people where they are angry, and disband troops you cannot pay.
  7. End the day (space), or let the days run. The war lasts three years: hold as much land as you can.
