Italia

A thousand years of noble families, on one battlefield.

Italy as ISTAT drew it for the census of 1871: the old provinces of the Italian states (Terra di Lavoro, Capitanata, the Calabrias, the Papal legations) and the districts inside them, the lands that families fought over for centuries before. Each area has its 1871 population, which pays taxes and gives recruits, and its real elevation: the Alps and the Apennines are slow to cross and easy to hold, and Sicily and Sardinia are reached by sea. You lead one family; the Lilienstern engine leads the others, in your browser.

How to play

  1. Click an area to see who holds it, its terrain, castles, wealth and people in 1871, the troops in it, and the decisions you can take there: settle empty land next to yours, govern, fortify, raise troops (where you hold), or make war and peace with the family that holds it.
  2. Click one of your troops (on the map, or in a province's card), then click where it should go. It takes the quickest way: hills and mountains are slow, horses cannot cross the high mountains, and the islands are a long sea crossing away (Sicily from Reggio, Sardinia from the nearest port). You can give orders as often as you like; decisions are limited to a few a day.
  3. Troops that meet enemies stop and fight, every day, until one side is gone: each troop hits the enemy it is best against. Defenders are helped by hills, forests, castles and their own land.
  4. A troop standing in a province with no enemy troops occupies it: day by day its realm's hold grows and the old holder's fades, and the province changes hands. Heavy troops take places fastest; castles slow them down.
  5. Every area pays funds and gives recruits each day, by its people: Napoli, Milano or Palermo are worth many Alpine valleys. Troops cost funds to keep; a family that cannot pay sees its troops melt away.
  6. End the day (space), or let the days run. The war lasts five years: hold as much of Italy as you can.
